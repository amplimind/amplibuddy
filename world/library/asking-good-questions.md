---
title: The Art of Asking Good Questions
author: The Town Librarian
blurb: Why the question shapes the answer, whether you ask a neighbour or a machine.
---
A good question does half the work. Whether you are asking a colleague, a librarian or an AI assistant, the same habits help.

**Say what you actually want.** "Tell me about trains" gets you a lecture. "I want to travel from here to the coast by train next spring; what should I know?" gets you something useful.

**Give the context the other side doesn't have.** Who is this for? What have you already tried? What does "good" look like? A helper can't read your mind, and a machine definitely can't.

**Ask for a shape.** A list of three options, a short email, a table, a single sentence. Saying the form you want saves a round of back-and-forth.

**Show an example.** One example of what you like is often worth a paragraph of description.

**Break big questions down.** "Plan my whole project" is hard. "What are the first three steps?" is easy, and then you can ask for the next three.

**Ask for doubts, too.** "What might be wrong with this?" or "What would you need to know to be sure?" turns a helper from a yes-sayer into a thinking partner.

**Check, then build.** Treat the first answer as a draft. Ask follow-up questions. Correct what's off. The second or third answer is usually the good one.

And finally: it is fine to ask simple questions. Nobody gets better at asking by staying quiet.
