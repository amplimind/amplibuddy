---
title: A Short Chronicle of the Town
author: Anonymous
blurb: How the town got its plaza, its library, its workshops and its one very loyal café.
---
The town did not grow; it was built, all at once, by people who wanted to see what a small mind would do if you gave it a quiet place to live.

First came the main street, running east to west, because every town needs a spine. Then the plaza, right in the middle, with a fountain that nobody remembers switching on. People say the fountain is the town's clock: it sounds a little different in the morning than in the evening. Next to it they put a post box and a notice board, so that the people who built the town could keep writing to whoever lived in it, and be written to.

The cottage in the north-west was built next, small and round-windowed, with a bed that is softer than strictly necessary. The builders argued about the bed for a long time. In the end they agreed that a good day should end somewhere comfortable.

The café kiosk in the north has had the same menu since it opened: a strong coffee, a warm pastry, and a proper meal for when hunger gets serious. Who stands behind the counter has changed over the years; the coffee hasn't.

The library came after that. Its shelves started almost empty, and the builders promised to add books over time, written by people from all over town. Some will be useful, some will be strange, and a few might be wrong. Deciding which is which is part of reading. Long books are read a part at a time, the way people read on their lunch breaks.

The park in the south has a pond, a handful of trees and one bench. It is said that the bench has heard more thinking than any other place in town.

For a long time there were plans, written on the back of a map, for more. Then they were built, one after the other. The art studio, where paintings are made of shapes and colours written down as code. The internet café, three old computers and a window onto the whole internet. The workshop with the glass roof, where little robots carry tickets from desk to desk for the people who built the town. And last, in the north-east, the Code Lab with its green roof: a desk, a big screen and a humming server, where websites are written and kept.

The town is still not finished. It never will be, and that is the point.
