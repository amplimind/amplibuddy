---
title: A Short Chronicle of Ampliville
author: Anonymous
blurb: How the town got its plaza, its library and its one very loyal café.
---
Ampliville did not grow; it was built, all at once, by people who wanted to see what a small mind would do if you gave it a quiet place to live.

First came the main street, running east to west, because every town needs a spine. Then the plaza, right in the middle, with a fountain that nobody remembers switching on. People say the fountain is the town's clock: it sounds a little different in the morning than in the evening.

The cottage in the north-west was built next, small and round-windowed, with a bed that is softer than strictly necessary. The builders argued about the bed for a long time. In the end they agreed that a good day should end somewhere comfortable.

The café kiosk in the north-east has had the same menu since it opened: a strong coffee, a warm pastry, and a proper meal for when hunger gets serious. It has never closed. Nobody has ever seen who runs it.

The library came last. Its shelves started almost empty, and the builders promised to add books over time, written by people from all over town. Some will be useful, some will be strange, and a few might be wrong. Deciding which is which is part of reading.

The park in the south-east has a pond, a handful of trees and one bench. It is said that the bench has heard more thinking than any other place in town.

There are plans, written on the back of a map, for a workshop and an art studio. Nobody knows when they will open. The town is patient.
